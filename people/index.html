Lab Members

Dr. Mark V. Albert

Director · Chair of Information Science · Associate Professor

Mark.Albert (at) unt.edu

  • Google Scholar
  • Full profile & teaching

Prof. Albert uses machine learning to automate the collection and inference of clinically useful health information. His work in wearable sensing and gait analysis has improved how outcomes are measured for people with Parkinson's disease, stroke, amputation and cerebral palsy, and the lab now also builds language and imaging models for health. He chairs the Department of Information Science and is an Associate Professor of Computer Science and Engineering with a joint appointment in Biomedical Engineering.

Fahmida is a PhD student in Computer Science and Engineering who worked with Dr. Albert from Fall 2024 to Fall 2026 as a graduate research assistant, and taught as a teaching assistant for CSCE 1040 and CSCE 4115. Her research is in health informatics and clinical natural language processing: with Shriners Children’s, she uses language models on pediatric electronic health records to estimate pain severity, predict the likelihood of surgery and assess fall risk in children with cerebral palsy (IEEE ICHI 2026).

Himanshu Sharma

PhD Candidate · Graduating Summer 2027

himanshusharma (at) my.unt.edu

  • Google Scholar

Himanshu joined UNT in Fall 2018, completed his MS in Computer Science in Spring 2020, and began the PhD program in Fall 2020. His dissertation develops a multimodal temporal fusion framework, using cross-modal attention, for automated echocardiogram report generation, building on earlier work compressing EKG signals with deep autoencoders. He co-authored a 2023 Sensors paper on a low-power wireless system that predicts sudden cardiac arrest on an embedded NVIDIA Jetson, and contributes to the lab’s work on explaining language models with counterfactuals. His interests include computational neuroscience and natural language processing.

Ian Abeyta

PhD Student, Information Science · Graduating Summer 2027

  • LinkedIn

Co-advised with Dr. Heejun Kim. Ian holds an MPH and is pursuing a PhD in Information Science (Health Informatics), applying machine learning to human biomechanics and clinical data. As a graduate research team lead at UNT since 2024, he guides undergraduate and graduate students building databases, data pipelines and machine learning models that analyze real-time patient data. In UNT’s DVXR Lab he built a real-time digital twin of human movement in Unity 3D, driven by six leg-worn inertial sensors, with models that classify movement form.

Before his PhD, Ian spent several years in healthcare analytics and business intelligence, most recently at CHRISTUS Health and Unifeye Vision Partners. He is also a Certified Strength and Conditioning Specialist (CSCS).

Muhammad Zubair Hasan

PhD Student · Lab Manager

  • Home Page
  • Google Scholar
  • GitHub

Zubair is a PhD student in Computer Science and Engineering, a research assistant working on AI for healthcare, and the lab manager. His research covers compact vision-language models, medical image segmentation and recognizing toddlers’ physical activity with machine learning. He co-authored a survey of small vision-language models in Information Fusion (2026) and DFUSegNet, a diabetic foot ulcer segmentation network, in Knowledge-Based Systems (2025).

Before UNT, he spent more than three years as a data scientist in industry, at Celloscope and Kona Software Labs, and did research in the DSP Lab at BUET. He holds a BSc in Computer Science and Engineering from the Military Institute of Science and Technology (MIST). His teams were runners-up in the IEEE Video and Image Processing Cup 2020 and placed first on the live leaderboard of the Diabetic Foot Ulcer Challenge 2021.

Namratha Urs

PhD Candidate · Graduating Summer 2027

NamrathaUrs (at) my.unt.edu

  • Google Scholar
  • ORCID
  • GitHub
  • LinkedIn

Namratha works across computational neuroscience and natural language processing. In the lab she showed how the early visual and auditory systems can be “derived” from an efficient coding of natural scenes and sounds, publishing a primer and accessible Jupyter notebook that derives neural receptive fields from grayscale, color and audio input (Artificial Intelligence Review, 2022) and presenting the work at the Society for Neuroscience meeting. She also does discourse-processing research in UNT’s Human Intelligence and Language Technologies Lab and contributes to the lab’s work on explaining language models with counterfactuals.

Since 2022 she has been a software developer at Fermilab, after internships there, working on GlideinWMS, distributed-computing software that runs physics workloads on grids, clouds and supercomputers. She has also spent a summer at Argonne National Laboratory.

Saba Yousefian Jazi

PhD Candidate · Graduating Summer 2027

sabayousefianjazi (at) my.unt.edu

  • Google Scholar
  • GitHub
  • LinkedIn

Saba is a Computer Science and Engineering PhD candidate. Her dissertation develops physiologically appropriate efficient-coding models of spontaneous neural activity in the LGN and V1 and its role in binocular vision, extended to audio, images and video. She has also worked on markerless tumor tracking in lung imaging, multispectral image analysis, point-cloud clustering and an attention-based U-Net for autonomous lane detection.

Before UNT, at the University of Isfahan, she was first author of an emotion-aware music recommender system (Multimedia Tools and Applications, 2021). In Spring 2026 she mentored four senior capstone teams in computer science.

Sekhar Lanka

Part-time PhD Student · Senior AI Engineer, Microsoft

  • LinkedIn

Sekhar is a part-time PhD student in Computer Science and Engineering, focusing on artificial intelligence, machine learning and autonomous agents. Alongside his PhD he is a senior AI engineer and AI solution architect at Microsoft, where he designs enterprise AI platforms built on agentic and generative AI: multi-agent systems, retrieval-augmented generation, knowledge extraction and human-in-the-loop automation, applied especially to finance operations. He brings more than two decades of software engineering experience, including twelve years as a lead application developer at ADP.

In April 2026 he co-led, with Dr. Albert, a hands-on UNT training on using AI tools to improve the quality, productivity and enjoyment of teaching and scholarly work, and his student startup team VeriWell was a finalist in UNT’s 2025 iUNT Innovation Pitch Competition.
