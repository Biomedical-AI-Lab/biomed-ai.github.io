Mark V. Albert directs the Biomedical AI Lab. He is Chair of the Department of Information Science at the University of North Texas and an Associate Professor of Computer Science and Engineering, with a joint appointment in Biomedical Engineering. From 2021 to 2025 he was Associate Chair for Graduate Studies in Computer Science and Engineering, a period in which the department’s graduate program grew more than sixfold.

His research applies machine learning to clinical care, especially rehabilitation and wearable sensing: detecting falls and recognizing activity with phones and wearables, and scoring movement quality for clinicians. As an advisor to Shriners Children’s on gait analysis, he created the Shriners Gait Index, a single summary measure of gait quality for children with cerebral palsy. The lab also builds language and imaging models for health and humanitarian applications.

Before 2019 he was an Associate Professor of Computer Science at Loyola University Chicago, where he directed the Pervasive and Ambient Computing Lab and the Theoretical Neuroscience Lab. He was a postdoctoral researcher at Northwestern University and the Rehabilitation Institute of Chicago (now Shirley Ryan AbilityLab) with Konrad Kording and Arun Jayaraman, and an adjunct faculty member in Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation at Northwestern until 2022.

Beyond the classroom

  • Created and co-directs the AI Summer Research Program, which since 2020 has engaged 239 students in 75 projects with about 9 faculty advisors each summer. He ran a similar summer program at Loyola University Chicago from 2015 to 2019 (104 students, 34 projects).
  • Created and leads the CSE Seminar, taught every semester since Spring 2022.
  • Created the Data Science undergraduate program at Loyola University Chicago, 2018–2019.
  • Co-organized Chicago’s ASA DataFest, 2016–2019, and led the statistics block of Medical Decision Making for Northwestern University Feinberg School of Medicine students, 2015–2016.
  • Taught a 20-hour lecture series, Machine Learning to Human Learning, for researchers at Sapienza University of Rome, 2015.
  • Brain Geography mini-course, a neuroscience outreach effort that reached 465 students, 2006–2017.
