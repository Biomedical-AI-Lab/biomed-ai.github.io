Research in the Biomedical AI Lab

We build machine learning that clinicians can use: models that score movement, detect falls, read clinical text and interpret medical images. Below are a few highlights from each area.

  Movement & Gait

Movement and Gait Intelligence

Focus: Turning instrumented gait, balance and prosthetic data into single, interpretable scores clinicians can act on.

The Shriners Gait Index: a deep autoencoder summary of gait quality

Journal of Biomechanics, 2024 · IEEE ICHI, 2025

An autoencoder compresses whole-body gait data from children with cerebral palsy into one score of gait quality, so surgeons can compare outcomes before and after treatment. A cross-site study validated the index across Shriners Children's hospitals.

cerebral palsyautoencodersclinical outcomes
[Paper]

Machine learning in marker-based clinical gait analysis

Bioengineering, 2025

A scoping review of how machine learning is used in clinical motion-capture gait analysis, from classification and phenotyping to outcome prediction, and where the field needs better validation.

reviewmotion capturegait
[Paper]

Predicting Parkinson's motor symptoms from force plates

IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics, 2022

Machine learning on short quiet-standing recordings estimates UPDRS motor scores, pointing to a quick, low-cost balance test for tracking Parkinson's disease.

Parkinson's diseasebalanceforce plates

  Wearables

Wearable and Mobile Health Sensing

Focus: Phones and wearable sensors that recognize activity, detect falls and give a voice to people who cannot speak.

Wearable gesture recognition for motor-impaired users

IJCAI, 2024 · ECML PKDD, 2026

Few-shot continual learning, and now knowledge-attentive representation learning (KARL), let a wearable sensor learn new, user-defined hand gestures from motor-impaired individuals, building on the lab's gesture-to-speech app.

gesture recognitionfew-shot learningassistive tech
[Paper]

Fall classification with mobile phones

PLoS ONE, 2012

One of the early demonstrations that a phone's accelerometer, with machine learning, can detect and classify falls, later extended to real-world falls in people with amputations and to wearable airbags after stroke.

fall detectionsmartphonesrehabilitation
[Paper]

  Language Models

Language Models for Health and Humanitarian Text

Focus: Fine-tuning, probing and evaluating language models on clinical records and on high-stakes humanitarian text.

Instruction-tuned language models for pediatric fall risk

IEEE ICHI, 2026

An instruction fine-tuned model, trained iteratively with generated clinical texts, assesses fall risk from the records of children with cerebral palsy, with related work on pain assessment tuned to a single hospital system.

EHRLLM fine-tuningpediatrics

Cross-lingual stability and bias in humanitarian NLP

LREC, 2026

Measures how instruction-tuned models change their judgments across Russian, Ukrainian and English prompts when classifying human-rights violations, with companion work on conflict forecasting and peacebuilding reports.

multilingualbiassocial good

LIME-LLM: probing models with fluent counterfactuals

arXiv, 2026

Explains language-model predictions with fluent, meaningful counterfactual edits instead of the broken text that classic perturbation methods produce.

explainabilitycounterfactuals
[Paper]

  Multimodal AI

Medical Imaging and Multimodal AI

Focus: Multi-agent and multimodal systems that read medical images and write about them.

Multi-agent reasoning for radiology visual question answering

ACM/IEEE JCDL, 2025

Specialized agents divide up complex radiology questions, retrieving evidence and checking each other, with a companion multi-agent framework for generating full radiology reports.

radiologyagentsVQA

Small vision-language models: a survey

Information Fusion, 2026

A survey of compact vision-language models, their architectures, training and efficiency trade-offs, and where they fit when large models are impractical, including clinical settings.

vision-language modelsefficiencysurvey

  Foundations

Brain-Inspired Learning

Focus: Efficient-coding models of how the early visual and auditory systems learn, the lab's computational-neuroscience roots.

Innate visual learning through spontaneous activity

PLoS Computational Biology, 2008

Shows that the brain's spontaneous activity before birth can act as training data, letting efficient-coding models develop visual receptive fields before the eyes open.

Neural receptive fields from images and audio: a notebook

Artificial Intelligence Review, 2022

An accessible survey and Jupyter notebook that derives visual and auditory receptive fields from natural inputs, for anyone curious about the link between computing and neuroscience.
