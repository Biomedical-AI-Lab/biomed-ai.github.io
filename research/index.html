Research

We build machine learning methods that clinicians can use: models that score movement, detect falls, read clinical text and interpret medical images. Our work is organized into four themes, and most projects cross more than one.

Movement & Gait Intelligence Scoring and classifying movement from instrumented gait, prosthetic and balance data. Wearable & Mobile Health Sensing Phones and wearables that detect falls and recognize activity and hand gestures. Language Models for Health & Humanitarian Text Language models on medical records, and on conflict and human-rights text. Medical Imaging & Multimodal AI Radiology report generation, visual question answering and vision-language models.

Movement & Gait Intelligence

We turn instrumented gait, prosthetic and balance data into single, interpretable scores of movement quality. This includes the Shriners Gait Index, an autoencoder-based summary of gait for children with cerebral palsy, validated across clinical sites; data-driven phenotyping of hemiplegic gait; and composite scores of prosthetic performance after lower-limb amputation.

Wearable & Mobile Health Sensing

We use phones and wearable sensors to recognize activity and posture, detect falls and respond in real time, and measure how well everyday activities are performed. Our work has covered Parkinson’s disease, incomplete spinal cord injury, transfemoral amputation, stroke and toddlers, and now includes wearable hand-gesture recognition for people who cannot speak.

Language Models for Health & Humanitarian Text

We fine-tune and probe language models for clinical records, such as pediatric fall risk and pain assessment, and for humanitarian text, such as human-rights annotation, conflict forecasting and peacebuilding reports. We also study how to explain, distill and fairly evaluate these models.

Medical Imaging & Multimodal AI

We build multi-agent and multimodal systems for radiology report generation and visual question answering, and survey where small vision-language models and multimodal language models are headed in medicine.

Foundations

The lab’s roots include computational neuroscience: efficient-coding models of how early visual and auditory systems learn from natural input. See the neural efficient coding notebook and Prof. Albert’s teaching and book.
